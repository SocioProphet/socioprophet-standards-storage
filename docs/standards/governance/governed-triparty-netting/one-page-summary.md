# Governed Triparty Netting Fabric  One-Page Summary

## Core claim

The right interoperability primitive is not a bridge, not a registry, and not a messaging standard in isolation. It is a **governed triparty netting fabric**: a three-party clearing cell that can cancel circulatory gross flow, preserve proof and provenance, separate evidence from permission, and support release, refund, suppression, or export according to policy rather than mere confidence.

## Problem

Across blockchains, rollups, institutional ledgers, identity systems, and disclosure systems, the same failure appears repeatedly:
local records may be accurate while global truth remains delayed, hidden, policy-constrained, or only partially reconstructable.

That creates three recurring pathologies:
- unnecessary gross settlement where multilateral netting should be possible,
- strong evidence without legitimate permission to merge, release, or export,
- operationally useful confidence being mistaken for proof.

## Thesis

The minimal useful clearing object is a **triparty simplex**:

\[
\Delta^2 = [A,B,C]
\]

A pair only supports bilateral transfer. A filled triangle is the smallest object that supports genuine internal cancellation of cyclic demand.

But the real production object is not just a triangle. It is a **governed prism** built from:
- typed events and claims,
- scope and trust-domain boundaries,
- truth classes,
- policy bundles,
- action rights,
- proof artifacts.

## Governing rule

The system distinguishes four progressively stronger sets:
- what evidence supports,
- what policy admits,
- what proof can release,
- what may be exported into a wider scope.

This yields the constitutional rule:

**Evidence proposes. Policy disposes. Proof preserves the result. Export is stricter than local validity.**

## Why it matters

This architecture spans more than cross-chain value movement.
It covers:
- value netting,
- authority and role-bearing release,
- capability routing,
- governed identity,
- selective disclosure,
- proof-carrying export.

That makes it useful for crypto-native ecosystems, institutional ledgers, identity systems, and mixed settlement/disclosure environments.

## Why now

The surrounding ecosystems already expose fragments of this architecture:
- intent-based coordination,
- multi-party acceptance states,
- payment channels and escrows,
- proof and timeout discipline,
- capability-style tokenization,
- verifiable credentials and selective disclosure.

The missing layer is the **governed release constitution** that makes those pieces cohere.

## Near-term strategy

We do not need to replace existing ecosystems.
We layer a stronger control architecture onto existing rails.

Near-term:
- use current settlement and payment rails where they already exist,
- add governed admission, release, challenge, and export logic,
- prove the model through bounded integrations.

## First bounded product

The first product is not all interoperability.
It is a **Governed Coordination Kernel Toolkit**:
- canonical lifecycle,
- canonical bundles,
- replay and timeout rules,
- trace and proof model,
- adapter interface contract,
- worked integrations.
